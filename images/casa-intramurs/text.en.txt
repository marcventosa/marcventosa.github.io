*Matosinhos, Porto*
Taller Belén Ramos
Three workshop houses for artists
/
The sculptor eagerly recognized the house that Souto de Moura had had built decades ago. When he came across the estate where he would spend the next few summers, everything seemed scattered, as if they had been putting together pieces of walls, of roofs, of empty spaces and of orchards, picking them up one by one, as if mounting a wall with found stones. But little by little, with each alignment, with each new wall raised,a modulated volumes compass was discovered, recognizing the dynamic character of the narrow streets, roofs and boats of Matosinhos. The concrete walls seen, almost like hundred-year-old statues, showed the scars that the rainwater had been leaving, while the upper part was deformed to become an open channel, distributing the torrent of zinc roofs towards the orchards and the border forest. He was surprisedbeing received by an outdoor space that, without funnels, showed the door of the workshop where he would create his new collection. Through a gap, a distributor courtyard opened, a modern cloister, which gave access to the rest rooms of the estate. As if he had never left the narrow streets of Matosinhos, he was now discovering a small town that, without realizing it, would become homehis.

Arriving at a well-lit room, he settled in, before moving on to the living room. In that courtyard protected by walls, I already saw summer lunches with company. A discreet kitchen accompanied the space with a board that crossed the load-bearing wall, communicating with the exterior courtyard. The sculptor went to the last room. The workshop was wide and wasdrawn between two concrete wallss. He appreciated the texture of the formwork once inside.

This border was crossed by a large sliding door, made of sheet metal and memories of the industrial port, which he had to push hard. Once the threshold was crossed, a small era unfolded, flanked by the old orchardss that were drawn between the two walls. As he contemplated that scenario, hesurprise the cry of his new neighbor, a writer immersed in his novel, who also seemed captivated by the place. He insisted on recording the first impressions that this architecture had awakened in him.
