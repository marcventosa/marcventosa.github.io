*Matosinhos, Porto*
Taller Belén Ramos
Three houses-workshops for artists
/
The sculptor eagerly recognized the house that Souto de Moura had had built decades ago. When he came across the farm where he would spend the next few summers, everything seemed scattered, as if they had been putting together pieces of walls, roofs, empty spaces and orchards, picking them up one by one, like someone who mounts a wall with found stones. But little by little, with each alignment, with each new wall raised, a compass of volumes was discoveredmodulated, recognizing the dynamic character of the alleys, roofs and boats of Matosinhos. The concrete walls seen, almost like centuries-old statues, showed the scars that the rainwater had been leaving, while the upper part deformed to become an open channel, distributing the stream of zinc roofs towards the orchards and the border forest. He was surprised to be greeted by an outdoor space that, withoutfunnels, he showed the door of the workshop where he would create his new collection. Through a gap, a distributor courtyard opened up, a modern cloister, which gave access to the rest rooms of the estate. As if he had never left the alleys of Matosinhos, he was now discovering a small town that, without realizing it, would become his home.

Arrived at a well-lit room, he settled in, before moving on to the living roomof being. In that courtyard protected by walls, I could already see summer lunches with company. A discreet kitchen accompanied the space with a counter that crossed the load-bearing wall, communicating with the outside courtyard. The sculptor went to the last room. The workshop was spacious and laid out between two concrete walls. He appreciated the texture of the formwork once inside.

This border was crossed by a large sliding door, made ofsheet metal and memories of the industrial port, which he had to push hard. Once the threshold was crossed, a small era unfolded, flanked by the old orchards that were traced between the two walls. As he contemplated that scenario, he was struck by the cry of his new neighbor, a writer immersed in his novel, who also seemed captivated by the place. He insisted on recording the first impressions that this architecture had aroused in- Him.
